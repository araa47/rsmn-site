01 — The bill

₹5.3 crore a year. Most of it invisible.

A hotel doesn’t waste energy in one place. It wastes it a room at a time, a chiller-hour at a time — and none of it shows up until the bill.

₹11.50

per unit all-in: ₹9.40 energy, ₹608/kVA demand, 5% tax — the HT-III commercial tariff.

What the hotel already does

None of this is broken. It is just not per room.

  • Key card at the doorPower cuts when the card is pulled — but guests leave a spare in the slot, and half the rooms get bypassed.
  • Manual thermostatThe guest sets 18°C and the room runs there all night.
  • Chillers on a timetableSet when the building opened. They still start and stop at the same hours whether the hotel is full or a third empty.
  • Housekeeping by phoneRoom status is a phone call, not a signal.

How we know a room is empty

mmWave radar, not PIR. PIR reacts to movement and can be fooled by a warm draft — a guest reading in a chair or asleep drops off it, and the room gets cooled for nobody. mmWave holds a still person, works in the dark, sits behind a plastic panel and has no lens.

All ten are kilowatt-hours. Demand-charge reduction and moving laundry off the peak are worth money too — deliberately not counted, so the model can only under-promise.

03 — Scope

What we automate.

One layer runs the building in states — occupied, vacant, arriving, servicing. One layer knows which state it is actually in.

Occupancy cooling

mmWave radar knows empty from still. Setback when it’s empty, the guest’s setpoint when it isn’t.

Pre-arrival from the PMS

Cool 40 minutes before check-in, not all morning.

Check-out reset

AC, lights, TV and curtains reset in one event.

Shade before the sun

Blinds close ahead of direct sun and open after it.

Doors, windows, bathrooms

AC pauses on an open balcony door. Exhaust follows humidity.

Housekeeping status

Occupied, ready, service or DND — from the room, not a call.

Daylight dimming

The lobby dims as the sun climbs.

A real night state

Corridors at 30%, up on presence, never dark on a guest path.

Banquet from the BEO

Cool 45 minutes before doors, not three hours.

Service-period scenes

Breakfast, lunch, bar, close — light and temperature by service.

Pool, facade, landscape

On solar time. Off after the last arrival.

Chiller sequencing

Sub-metering

Three things to check

  2. 02

  3. 03

    Detect

  4. 04

    Booking pace, arrivals and weather become a 72-hour forecast. Cool the floors you will sell.

  1. Day 1

    Rooms come online. Every room is compared with every other room.

  2. Day 3

  3. Week 2

    The first plant drift: a chiller, pump or AHU against its own past.

  4. Month 1

    The first measured report, against the baseline you agreed.

  5. Month 3

    Summer and monsoon behaviour modelled. The system starts predicting.

Measurement

How we prove it

Savings claims are where these projects lose credibility. Ours are reported the boring way.

  • A weather-normalised baseline agreed before switch-on (IPMVP Option C).
  • Avoided kWh and rupees per floor, outlet and plant item.
  • Temperature excursions and complaints next to the savings. Savings from discomfort don’t count.
  • The months that miss are published too.

09 — Guardrails

What we won’t do.

A pitch is a list of promises. These four don’t move, even when moving them would make the numbers look better.

Fire, lifts and emergency lighting stay with certified systems. We read from them.

Won’t trade comfort

10 — Questions

The questions that follow the price.

What does it cost?

₹1.2–2.2 crore for a 200-key retrofit, including hardware and commissioning, excluding civil work and GST. At the next AC or BMS replacement the marginal cost is roughly half. The audit fee is fixed and credited against implementation.

How disruptive is it?

Ninety minutes a room, in vacant inventory, nine to five, one floor at a time. No floor closes. Plant work happens in low-occupancy windows.

Do we have to replace the air-conditioners?

No. Controllers and gateways work with existing splits and FCUs through IR, Modbus or dry contacts. We audit before recommending anything.

Will it work with our PMS and BMS?

Reservations and arrivals come from the PMS API; plant data from the BMS over BACnet or Modbus. If the BMS can’t expose a reading, we clamp a meter at the panel.

Who maintains it?

Your engineering team, with a dashboard, training and a runbook. We can retain for monthly tuning and on-site support. The system is open, so you aren’t stuck with us.

What if the internet goes down?

Nothing a guest would notice. Control and schedules run on the local server; only remote dashboards pause.

What about guest data?

Analytics run on room numbers against anonymous profiles. No cameras or microphones in rooms. Held on site under the DPDP Act, never resold.

What’s the catch?

Time and access. A baseline needs a fortnight, installs need vacant rooms, and somebody on your side has to own the number. If those aren’t available, this doesn’t work — and we’ll say so in the first meeting.
