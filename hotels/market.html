01 — The landscape

Five things you can buy.
None of them joins up.

A hotel buys plant control, room control, analytics and gadgets from four different markets. Each is good at its slice. None of them closes the loop from the booking to the bill.

01 ₹2.8–5.2 crore
200 keys

The BMS major.

Chiller plant, AHUs, pumps, towers, meters, alarms and trends, on BACnet or Modbus. The right tool for a central plant, and the only segment that will sequence it properly.

What you get
Dense I/O, plant sequencing, alarm history, cybersecurity hardening, commissioning and a warranty.
Where it stops
No guest-room UX, no keypads, no PMS logic. Licensed per point or seat. Delivered by a certified integrator, so a setpoint change becomes a purchase order.

Names you’ll meet: Schneider EcoStruxure, Siemens Desigo, Honeywell, Johnson Controls, Delta Controls; Indian challengers such as EnSmart.

02 ₹87.5k–2.5L
a room

The luxury GRMS.

A room controller, keypad, occupancy sensing and a thermostat that sets back when the room is empty. INNCOM, Schneider’s room controllers, Lutron, Crestron and KNX all play here.

What you get
Room scenes, keycard/PIR logic, DND/MUR, curtains and a PMS link if you pay for it.
Where it stops
No plant control, no chiller optimisation, no portfolio analytics. Proprietary unless KNX or Modbus. Per-room hardware and programming.

Names you’ll meet: Honeywell INNCOM, Schneider SpaceLogic, Lutron Hospitality, Crestron, Control4, KNX vendors.

03 ₹35k–75k
a room

The midscale GRMS.

The same idea at a lower price: a wired room controller, card switch, thermostat and a few panels. The workhorse of midscale Indian hotels, and the honest comparator for our price.

What you get
Occupancy setback, keycard control, a few scenes, a local supplier and a standard warranty.
Where it stops
Little or no analytics, no PMS-driven pre-cooling, no plant view. The room saves energy, but nobody can prove how much or why.

Names you’ll meet: INNCOM, Legrand/Bticino, Indian RCU and panel vendors, regional ELV integrators.

04 €4.99
a room a month

The energy dashboard.

Meters, bill audit, benchmarking, alerts and measurement and verification. Useful, and often the fastest way to find the leak. But it is a report, not a control system.

What you get
Visibility, exception alerts, a monthly number and an evidence trail for the owner.
Where it stops
Mostly read-only. It can tell you the plant is drifting; it cannot reset the setpoint, cool the arriving room or turn off the AC after checkout.

Names you’ll meet: Schneider Resource Advisor, Zenatix (now Schneider Electric), Hatch, Enertiv, Aquicore, Sens.

05 ₹15k–27.5k
a room

The wireless retrofit.

BLE mesh panels, a card switch, a thermostat and a PIR, installed room by room. Cheap, fast, and reversible. The hotel keeps its wiring and its plant.

What you get
A room that sets back when empty, without opening a wall or shutting a floor.
Where it stops
No PMS integration, no plant view, no M&V, no report. A pile of rooms that save something, with no way to prove it.

Names you’ll meet: BLE Mesh and Zigbee vendors, regional retrofit firms, Home Assistant installers, online hardware sellers.

06 ₹60k–1.1L a key
₹1.2–2.2 crore for 200

Ambient Form: the overlay that closes the loop.

Keep the plant and its warranty. Add per-room control, a PMS link, a meter reading and a report that reconciles the three. One local hub per property, not a server per floor. The hotel owns the hardware, the data and the automations.

What we replace
The gap between the BMS, the room controller and the dashboard. Not the BMS itself, and not the chiller.
What we keep
Your plant, your PMS, your locks, your network where it is sound. We add the layer that reads and acts.
Where it stops
Life-safety stays certified. A new-build 300-key with brand standards may want a single BMS contract. See the limits.

What we don’t sell: a per-point licence, a 12-month shutdown, a proprietary server, or a saving we cannot meter.

The gap is the loop.

The BMS knows the plant. The GRMS knows the room. The PMS knows the booking. The dashboard knows the bill. None of them knows that room 412 checked out at 11:04, the AC is still at 18°C at 3pm, and the next guest lands at 6. Closing that loop is not a new chiller. It is a layer.

A BMS is priced per point. A hotel has thousands.

Every meter, sensor, room and alarm is a line item, and every added point can add a licence. That is why the quote scales with the building rather than the saving. The overlay is priced per room and per outcome, and a new meter is a configuration, not a variation order.

04 — Why it’s cheaper

Why ours costs less.

We are not cheaper than a wireless kit, because a wireless kit is not the same product. We are roughly a third of the five-year cost of a BMS-led programme and around half of a luxury KNX room, with the room, PMS and report included.

01

No per-point licence.

Every meter, sensor, room and alarm is a point in a BMS quote, and the front end is often licensed per point or seat. Our hub is bought once. Adding a meter is a configuration, not a variation order.

02

No new cabling by default.

The plant keeps its BACnet or Modbus backbone. The room uses what is there: Wi-Fi, Zigbee, BLE, IR, relay or the existing KNX bus. A floor can be done without closing the hotel.

03

Keep the plant and its warranty.

We do not touch the chiller, the AHU sequence or the fire system. We read and, where the BMS allows it, write setpoints. The plant contractor’s warranty stays intact, and so does the operator’s approval.

04

One hub, not a server per floor.

A BMS carries a server, a front end, a licence and an integrator. A GRMS carries a controller per room. Our property hub is one box, and the rooms are software on it.

05

Rooms are software.

A new rate plan, a new PMS field or a new checkout rule is a change on the hub, not a firmware project. The room controller stays; the logic moves.

06

Modular by floor.

Pilot one floor, prove the saving, then roll. The capex is staged and the risk is bounded. A BMS-led programme is usually all or nothing, with a 12–24 month clock.

Measured, not promised.

Savings are reported against a metered baseline and a comfort guardrail. Temperature excursions sit next to the saving, every month. If the saving comes from a warmer room, it does not count.

Open at the boundary.

06 — The endless part

Why it doesn’t go stale.

A hotel is never finished: a new tower, a new PMS, a new tariff, an EV charger, a water meter, a restaurant. A closed system ends at the scope document. An open layer can take the next change without a new vendor.

Your next meter is not a change order.

The hub speaks BACnet and Modbus where the plant exposes them, and MQTT, REST and ESPHome for everything else. A new room type is a template; a new tariff is a rate table; a new PMS is an integration, not a rip-and-replace.

That is what “endless” means here: the system is not sized to the building at handover. It is sized to the next change. It also has a boundary — life-safety stays certified, and a plant that needs replacing is a plant project.

“Endless” stops at life-safety.

Fire, lifts, smoke control, pressurisation and protection stay on certified systems. We monitor them read-only, or not at all. A hotel is not a lab, and an overlay that touches life-safety is not an overlay.

07 — The honest part

When to choose
someone else.

An overlay is not a religion. These are the jobs we would hand back, and the numbers on this page that need your bill before they mean anything.

01 New-build, 300+ keys, brand standards.

If the operator names approved BMS and GRMS vendors, and the plant is being commissioned from scratch, a single contracted BMS is the right answer. We can be the analytics and room layer on top, not the prime.

02 Life-safety and mission-critical plant.

Fire, lifts, smoke control, pressurisation and electrical protection stay certified and untouched. We read what is exposed; we do not control it.

03 The plant is the problem.

If the chiller is end-of-life or the pumps are undersized, an overlay measures the loss; it does not fix it. Pair it with a plant retrofit, or do not promise 20%.

04 No in-house engineering or IT.

A self-owned stack needs an owner: a chief engineer who can read an alarm, and an IT function that can segment a guest network. Without either, a supported GRMS with an SLA is lower-risk.

05 A 10-year single-contract warranty.

Some owners want one throat to choke for a decade. That is a legitimate requirement, and it points to a major. We will say so before you sign.

06 Cybersecurity with no owner.

Zigbee and Wi-Fi on a guest network need segmentation, patching and an asset register. If nobody owns that, adding devices makes the hotel less safe, not smarter.

The tariff needs your bill, not our model.

The hotel proposal on this site models ₹11.50 a unit — an industrial-tariff number. Under MERC’s March 2026 order, a Maharashtra commercial hotel connection is closer to ₹19.70 all-in. At ₹19.70, the same 4.6 GWh costs about ₹9.1 crore. If the bill is ₹5.3 crore, either the hotel is on an industrial tariff, or it uses about 55 kWh per occupied room-night rather than 94. One year of bills settles which hotel you are.

20% is the top of the credible range.

Room controls alone are typically 5–10 points of the whole bill. Plant scheduling, setpoints and fault correction add 3–6; lighting adds 2–3. Plan on 10–18%, and hold 20% for a metered pilot with plant measures. The 1.1–2.1 year payback on the proposal page is arithmetically right at 20% — the 20% is the assumption to defend.

09 — Sources & method

Show the working.

Tariffs, vendor benchmarks, case studies and price guides, read on 6 November 2026. Where the only evidence is a vendor case study, the page says so.

What the words mean
BMS
The building system that runs the plant — chillers, pumps, air handling. It does not run the rooms.
GRMS
The room controller: keycard, thermostat, panels and the logic inside one room.
PMS
The hotel’s booking system — check-in, check-out, room status and the guest record.
BACnet / Modbus
The languages plant equipment uses to share data. If the BMS exposes them, we can read them.
M&V
Measurement and verification: proving a saving against a baseline, not just claiming it.
AMC
Annual maintenance contract — the yearly fee after the warranty ends.
