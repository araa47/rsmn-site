01 — The landscape

The five quotes you will get.

They are not five prices for one product. They are five different products, with different strengths and different failure modes. The first two sell finish, the third sells a curated catalogue, the fourth sells a remote control. We sell the layer underneath.

01 ₹15–30 lakh
3BHK

The premium integrator.

The finished room: bespoke keypads, lighting scenes, AV, blinds and security from one catalogue, installed and warranted by a dealer. This is the benchmark for how a smart home should feel.

What you get
Design-led hardware, commissioning, a single warranty, a showroom and a name to call.
Where it stops
The programming is dealer-authored. Retrofit often means new wiring. A keypad can cost more than a phone. The annual contract is 8–15% of the project.

Names you’ll meet: Crestron, Lutron, Basalte, Savant. In Mumbai, Trescent Lifestyles has built a business on this model since 2006; the lowest band on its enquiry form is ₹25–50 lakh.

02 ₹8–20 lakh
3BHK

The KNX / Basalte integrator.

An open bus with a luxury surface. KNX is a real standard, not a proprietary one, and a good integrator can mix Basalte, ABB and CORE keypads in one project. That is a genuine advantage over Crestron and Control4.

What you get
A robust wired bus, wide device choice, and — if the ETS file is handed over — any KNX professional can service it later.
Where it stops
The bus still has to be wired. Basalte keypads run ₹35,000–90,000 each before installation. Programming needs ETS6 Professional (€1,000) and a certified programmer, and many homeowners never receive the project file.

Names you’ll meet: Basalte, ABB, CORE, Gira, Jung, MDT, Theben; KNX-certified integrators in Mumbai and Pune.

03 ₹5–10 lakh
3BHK

The curated system at our price.

Control4 is a dealer-installed system with a curated catalogue. AHA Smart Homes publishes a ₹5 lakh complete-3BHK benchmark from two Mumbai experience centres. If you want one brand, one showroom and one warranty at our price, this is the honest alternative.

What you get
A single product line, a showroom, a known integration path and a dealer on the phone.
Where it stops
You are inside one catalogue. Adding a device outside it means a dealer visit and dealer-only Composer Pro programming. The ₹5 lakh is a benchmark, not a quote, and the scope is fixed to the package.

Names you’ll meet: Control4, AHA Smart Homes; builder packages that arrive with the flat and end at handover.

04 ₹1–2.4 lakh
3BHK

The DIY stack.

Aqara sensors, a couple of smart switches, an IR blaster, a Hue bridge and the app that came with each. Cheap to start, genuinely useful in a rental, and entirely yours — until something stops working.

What you get
Low entry cost, incremental purchases, no dealer, no contract, no wall chasing.
Where it stops
Four to six apps. Cloud accounts, including Hue’s mandatory account since 2023. Automations only as good as the time you put in. When a routine breaks at 11pm, you are the support desk.

Names you’ll meet: Aqara, Xiaomi, Philips Hue, Wiz, SwitchBot, Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa — and a different app for each.

05 ₹4.5–5.5 lakh
3BHK

Ambient Form: the open layer at the price of a curated one.

One layer over whatever hardware is already there. Not a catalogue and not a single app: a local hub that speaks the protocols, one interface, and a studio that designs the interior and the automation together. At ₹5 lakh we are 37–75% below the published KNX band and 67–83% below Crestron, without giving up the functions that matter.

What you get
Any brand that speaks a protocol, retrofit without opening walls by default, one app, local control, documentation, and the system in your name.
Where it stops
We are not a bespoke keypad manufacturer and not the right firm for a 10,000 sq ft villa with a plant room. We say so below.

What we don’t sell: a brand, a licence, a five-year lock-in, or a showroom you pay for.

The sixth option is your designer’s electrician.

It is also the quote you are most likely to receive. A smart switch and an app is not a system; it is a remote control with a nicer wall plate. There is no device schedule, no network plan, no scene design and no one to call after handover. If the quote does not include drawings, it is not an automation quote.

Illustrative splits, not an audit of any firm. Built from public list prices and typical project structures; the point is the shape, not the exact percentage. Your quote will differ — and that is what the walkthrough is for.

The part that isn’t hardware is the part you can’t take with you.

One Basalte keypad is ₹35,000–90,000 — 7–18% of our entire ₹5 lakh package, for one switch. It stays on the wall when you sell the flat. The programming does not. If the dealer holds the project file, the next owner needs that dealer, and so do you. Ask for the file before you sign — in any system, including ours.

04 — Why it’s cheaper

Why ours costs less.

We are not undercutting a like-for-like product. We removed the parts of the premium model that do not make the room work — and kept the parts that do.

01

No brand licence.

A Basalte keypad is ₹35,000–90,000 before it is wired. A Zigbee or Wi-Fi keypad that triggers the same scene is a fraction of that. We are not against the expensive keypad — we just don’t make it the price of entry.

02

No proprietary bus.

Crestron and Control4 want their wiring and their dealer. KNX wants a wired bus and an ETS programmer. We use what is already there: Wi-Fi, Zigbee, BLE, IR, RF and relays. A retrofit does not open a wall by default.

03

Existing hardware stays.

Your ACs, TVs, curtain motors, doorbell, camera and most switches can usually stay. We add the layer that coordinates them. Replacing a working device is the most expensive way to look modern.

04

One hub, not one subscription.

No per-device cloud plan, no per-room licence, no annual fee to keep the lights working. The hub sits in the home and the automations run on it. Support is optional because the system does not stop when a subscription does.

05

One studio, fewer trades.

Interior design, electrical intent, network and automation are drawn together. That removes the coordination tax — the site visits, the blame, the change orders and the “we thought the electrician was doing that”.

06

Modular scope.

Start at ₹2 lakh with light and presence. Add climate, shade, security or cinema later. The system is designed to be added to, not replaced, so the second phase costs less than the first.

Open is not an ideology.

It is a cost structure. When the hub speaks the protocol, the brand stops being the gatekeeper. That is why the same room can be done for a third of the price without giving up the functions that matter.

Local is not a hobby.

A cloud outage should not stop a door from opening or a light from coming on. The automations run on a hub in the house. The internet is an input, not a dependency.

Documented is not a favour.

You get the device schedule, the network map, the scene list and the project file. If you sell the flat, the system goes with it. If you leave us, someone else can pick it up.

06 — The endless part

Why it doesn’t go stale.

“Endless possibilities” is usually a brochure line. Here it is an architectural property: if a device exposes a protocol, it can join the system. You are not buying the last device the brand will ever make.

Your next device is not a change order.

The hub speaks Zigbee, Wi-Fi, BLE, IR, RF and relay, and can reach Modbus, MQTT, REST and ESPHome. Matter devices join directly. A device with a published API can be brought in with a small integration; a device with only a closed cloud cannot, and we will tell you before you buy it.

That is the difference between a system that is finished on handover day and one that keeps absorbing what you buy next: a new lock, a second AC, a meter, a projector, a sensor that costs less than lunch.

07 — The honest part

When to choose someone else.

A comparison page that says we win everything is a brochure. These are the jobs we would hand to someone else, and the reasons we would give.

01 A 10,000 sq ft villa with a plant room.

If the brief is Lutron HomeWorks, a dedicated rack, a full-time facility team and a single ten-year warranty, a specialist integrator is the right call. We can work alongside them; we should not pretend to be them.

02 The keypad is the project.

If the client wants bespoke metal or stone keypads, we can integrate them, but we don’t manufacture them. Buy the keypad you love; let us make it work with everything else.

03 No reliable network, no appetite to fix it.

The layer needs a network and a hub with power. In an old building with thick walls and one router, we quote the network first. A smart home on a bad Wi-Fi is an expensive demo.

04 A showroom and a 24/7 desk.

We are a studio, not a national service organisation. If the requirement is a four-hour on-site response at 3am, a large dealer with a contract is a better fit. We will say so before you sign.

09 — Sources & method

Show the working.

Prices move, dealers differ and a Mumbai quote is not a Delhi quote. These are the public references behind the page, with the date each was read.

What the words mean
Protocol
The language a device speaks. If it speaks one we know, it can join the system.
KNX
A wired standard for home control. Open, but it needs certified programming and a cable.
Zigbee / Wi-Fi / BLE
Three common wireless languages. We can speak all three, and most sensors use one of them.
Matter
A newer standard that lets devices from different brands work together.
AMC
Annual maintenance contract — the yearly fee after the warranty ends.
ETS
The software used to program a KNX system. The professional licence alone is about ₹1 lakh.
