01 — The landscape

Four things you can buy.
None of them runs the flat.

A host does not buy a system. They buy a lock, then a channel manager, then a noise sensor, then a manager. Each is good at its slice. None of them owns the flat between guests.

01 ₹3k–44k
per door

The smart lock.

Self check-in, a code per guest, no key handover. The category that made short-lets work. Igloohome’s offline PIN is the standout, because it works when the Wi-Fi doesn’t.

What you get
A door that opens without you, an audit trail and a battery warning.
Where it stops
It knows who may enter, not whether anyone is inside, how hot the room is, or that the AC has been on since checkout.

Names you’ll meet: igloohome, Yale, Godrej, Dorset, Openapp, Qubo, Sifely. Most Indian mortise locks expose no local API.

02 ₹10k–66k
a year

The channel manager.

Distribution, one inbox, automated messages, cleaner tasks, damage claims. A serious operator with four or more listings should pay for one. The mistake is expecting it to run the flat.

What you get
Bookings from many channels, a unified calendar, guest messaging and task lists.
Where it stops
No device control. Some plans charge per smart device beyond two, so the “integration” becomes another subscription.

Names you’ll meet: Guesty, Hostaway, Hospitable, Smoobu, Zeevou. Hospitable charges $5 per device beyond two per property.

03 ₹5.6k–18.7k
a year

The noise sensor.

A decibel monitor that texts you when a party starts. The good ones also estimate occupancy and smoking. None of them can turn the music down or cool the room before the next guest.

What you get
An alert, a history and something to show the society committee.
Where it stops
It alerts; it does not act. Stop paying and the monitoring stops. Cameras are banned indoors, so the sensor has to do the work alone.

Names you’ll meet: Minut, Party Squasher, NoiseAware. Minut is $5–15 per home a month; Party Squasher is $199 a year after year one.

04 20–30%
of revenue

The property manager.

Keys, cleaners, guest messages, the 1am call. In Goa, 42% of listings are professionally managed. A good manager is worth the fee; a bad one is an expensive WhatsApp group.

What you get
Humans on the ground, a cleaner roster, guest support and someone else’s phone number.
Where it stops
They usually bring their own lock and their own stack. You do not own the data, the automations or the relationship.

Names you’ll meet: local managers and co-hosts in Mumbai and Goa. Full management is 20–30% of rental income; co-hosts start around 10% of bookings.

05 ₹55k–95k capex
₹4.5k a year support

Ambient Form: the layer the slices are missing.

One local hub per flat. It talks to the lock, the AC, the presence sensor and the decibel meter, and it knows the booking. Checkout triggers the AC off, the cleaner task and the next arrival’s pre-cool. A guest gets one QR, not one app per device.

What we replace
The pile of device apps and their per-device subscriptions. The lock stays if it can be controlled; the AC stays in almost every case.
What we keep
Your channel manager, your cleaner, your listing. We are not a PMS and we do not want to be one.
Where it stops
We do not replace a manager’s 1am phone call, and we do not make an unlicensed flat legal. See the limits.

What we don’t sell: a camera, a microphone, a per-device plan, or a five-year contract.

The gap is the flat between guests.

The lock knows who enters. The PMS knows who booked. The noise sensor knows there is a party. None of them knows the AC is drying out an empty room, the cleaner is knocking on an occupied flat, or the next guest lands in 40 minutes and the flat is at 32°C. That is the job we take.

04 — Why it’s cheaper

Why ours costs less.

Our capex is not the lowest. Our five-year cost is, because the hardware is bought once and the intelligence lives on a hub you own — not on five plans that bill every month.

01

No per-device subscription.

Hospitable charges $5 per smart device beyond two. Igloohome charges $2 per lock a month. Minut and Party Squasher bill every year. We bill once for the install, then support is optional.

02

One hub, not five bridges.

The lock, sensors and AC controller join the same local hub. One thing to power, one thing to update, one thing to replace. Fewer boxes also means fewer apps and fewer accounts.

03

Existing hardware stays.

Most split ACs take an IR controller. A lock with a local API can stay. The door sensor, the camera at the entrance and the Wi-Fi you already have can often stay too. We replace the layer, not the flat.

04

One visit, one afternoon.

A one-flat install is a day, not a project. No chasing walls, no new distribution board, no building permission for wiring. The flat keeps working between bookings.

05

No markup on a manager’s stack.

A manager’s 20–30% buys human support, not hardware. If you keep a manager, the system should make their job smaller — fewer calls, fewer keys, fewer “the AC is stuck” visits.

06

Modular by flat.

Start with one flat and one lock. Add noise, AC or a second flat when the first is proven. You are not buying a portfolio licence before you have a portfolio.

Privacy is the spec.

No indoor camera. No microphone. Noise is a level, not a recording. Guest names stay in your booking system; the hub only needs the code, the dates and the temperature.

The flat works offline.

The code lives on the lock and the automations run on the hub. A dead router is an inconvenience, not a lockout. There is still a key, and a keysafe if the battery dies.

The review is the return.

Self check-in and an arrival at 24°C are the two things a guest notices before they notice the decor. Neither needs a new flat; both need the systems to talk.

06 — The endless part

Why it doesn’t go stale.

A closed kit is sized for the flat you bought it for. An open hub is sized for the lock, the meter or the second flat you have not bought yet.

The next device is not a new app.

Matter, Zigbee, Wi-Fi, BLE, IR, RF and relay are all in reach, and the hub can talk to MQTT, REST and ESPHome. A new lock pairs; a second AC gets another IR controller; a smart meter starts measuring the flat instead of guessing.

That is the difference between a kit that ends at the box and a system that absorbs the next purchase. It is also how a one-flat install becomes a four-flat operation without four separate apps.

07 — The honest part

When to choose someone else.

A short-let is a regulated business with a guest in the room. The technology is the easy part; these are the hard parts.

01 It is not a channel manager.

No OTA sync, no payments, no tax invoices, no damage claims. If you list on several channels, keep a PMS. We integrate with it or sit beside it; we do not replace it.

02 It is not insurance.

AirCover is a platform guarantee, not a policy. Commercial short-let use may fall outside a home policy. Get a quote for short-let cover before the first booking.

03 It is not a 24/7 guest line.

Automation removes the key handover, not the 2am call. If you want someone on the phone at 2am, that is a manager — and 20–30% of revenue is what that costs.

04 One hub is one point of failure.

We plan for it: offline codes, a mechanical key, a keysafe and a battery warning months ahead. But a hub is a device, and devices fail. The fallback is part of the design, not an afterthought.

Before the first booking: licence, NOC, police intimation.

In Mumbai, short-term letting needs a BMC licence, a society NOC and police intimation of guest details; the MCGM policy sets a 10-year validity and penalties of 50–100% of revenue. In December 2024 the Bombay High Court upheld a society’s right to restrict short-lets. Check with the ward office and the society before you spend on a lock. A better check-in does not fix an unlicensed flat.

What the words mean
PMS / channel manager
The software that manages bookings, messages and prices across the listing sites.
Per-device subscription
A monthly or yearly fee for each gadget or app, rather than one fee for the flat.
Zigbee / Wi-Fi / BLE
Three common wireless languages for sensors, locks and controllers.
IR
Infrared — the same signal as a remote. It is how most split ACs are controlled.
Offline code
A door code held on the lock itself, so it still works when the Wi-Fi is down.
DPDP
India’s data protection law. Guest IDs, lock logs and occupancy are personal data; the hub keeps them in the flat.
