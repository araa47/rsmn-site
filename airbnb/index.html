01 — The problems

Eight things every host already knows.

None of these are unusual. They are what running a flat as a hotel looks like when nobody is standing in it.

01

The door that doesn’t catch

The guest pulls it shut. It sits ajar. You find out from the cleaner, the neighbour, or the next guest.

02

Check-in at 1 a.m.

A delayed flight, and you are either awake or paying somebody to be.

03

The code that never changes

Same keybox code for two years — shared with every guest, cleaner and friend.

04

Three days of air-conditioning

Checkout at 11. The flat is empty until Friday. The compressor doesn’t know that.

05

The cleaner at the wrong door

No reliable signal that the guest actually left. So she knocks, waits, or goes home.

06

The five-star that became a four

32°C when they walked in. Everything else was perfect, and none of it counted.

07

The bill you can’t split

One meter, several flats, and no idea which stay left the geyser on.

08

Managing from another city

Every problem becomes a flight, a favour, or a review you can’t answer.

A hotel has staff in the building. A short-let has a guest, a lockbox and hope. The flat needs to be the staff.

02 — Scope

What the flat does by itself.

Six systems, one hub. Every one of them replaces a phone call you would otherwise take at an inconvenient hour.

Access & check-in

  • A unique code per booking, created at payment.
  • It expires at checkout — no shared number, ever.
  • The door locks itself when it closes.
  • Door-left-open alert, and a remote lock if it sticks.

Climate

  • Cooling starts 40 minutes before arrival, not all day.
  • Off at checkout. Back to 26.5°C until the next guest.
  • 24°C ceiling, with the guest free to go warmer.
  • Pauses when a window or balcony door opens.

Energy

  • A meter per flat, not per building.
  • kWh and rupees per stay, in the monthly report.
  • Geyser and washing machine on their own schedule.
  • An eco setting the guest can see and understand.

Turnaround

  • The cleaner is dispatched when the flat is actually empty.
  • Photo proof of the reset, on the way out.
  • Late checkout caught before the cleaner arrives.
  • Linen and consumables counted per turnover.

Guest

  • Arrival scene: lights, AC, Wi-Fi on a QR card.
  • Checkout reminder with the time and the bin.
  • Late checkout offered automatically if the calendar allows.
  • No app to download, no account to create.

Safety

  • A camera at the entrance only — never inside.
  • Smoke, gas and water-leak alerts to your phone.
  • Noise as a level, not a recording.
  • Battery, tamper and offline warnings before the guest complains.

04 — The premium

Why a smart flat rents for more.

Self check-in is one of the few amenities guests actively filter for. That changes who books, not just what they write afterwards.

  1. 01
    It is a filter, not a gadget

    Guests search for self check-in the way they search for parking. A listing without it is invisible to that search.

  2. 02
    The late arrival pays the most

    The 11 p.m. flight is the booking most hosts turn down. It is the easiest one to serve.

  3. 03
    A 5.0 protects the rate

    Most four-star reviews start at the door or the thermostat. Remove both failures and you stop buying back your rating with discounts.

  4. 04
    Rate, not discount

    A flat that never has a check-in problem does not need to drop its price to fill a Tuesday.

We won’t quote a study claiming 9%. The premium depends on your market, so it is a slider in the model below — set it to zero and the energy case still stands.

07 — Rollout

Two weeks, then a month of proof.

Your own flat, installed between two bookings. Nothing else changes until it earns its keep.

  1. 01Week 1–2

    Install

    Lock, hub, presence sensor, AC control, door sensor. One flat, one afternoon.

    Done between two bookings.

  2. 02Week 3–6

    Prove it

    Rate, reviews and kWh against your own last month — same flat, same season.

    It earns more or uses less — ideally both.

  3. 03Week 7–14

    Tune

    Setpoints, cleaning dispatch and pricing rules. The first month is noise; the second is a pattern.

    The exceptions get rarer, not more frequent.

  4. 04Month 3 onward

    Operate

    Monthly energy and review report, battery and filter checks, remote support when a door sticks.

    You stop thinking about it.

08 — Guardrails

What we won’t do.

A guest’s flat is not a shop floor. These four hold even when the data would be easier to collect the other way.

No camera inside

Entrance only, and disclosed in the listing. Nothing in a room a guest sleeps in.

No microphone

Noise is measured as a level for a party warning. Never recorded, never listened to.

No shared codes

One code, one booking, expiring at checkout. Cleaners and contractors get their own.

No lock-in

Open hub, standard locks, exportable data. If you sell the flat, the system goes with it.

09 — Questions

The things a host asks second.

What if the lock battery dies?

The lock warns at 20% and again at 10%, months before it matters. There is a key, and the code still works offline. Battery changes go into the same maintenance list as the AC filter.

What if the Wi-Fi goes down?

Codes live on the lock, so the guest still gets in. Presence, AC and lighting fall back to local schedules. Only remote alarms pause, and they queue.

Can a guest unplug the hub?

The lock is independent of the hub, so the flat still opens. The hub is on a secured supply; if it goes offline you get a warning, and the next guest’s code is already on the lock.

Do I have to replace the air-conditioners?

No. Splits and window units are controlled through IR or a relay, and the geyser through a switch. We audit what is there first.

Will guests find it creepy?

They see a keypad and a thermostat. There is no camera or microphone in the flat, and the listing says so. The sensors detect presence, not people.

Can I still meet guests if I want to?

Yes, and for some bookings you should. Self check-in is the default, not a rule — you can take over any stay from the app.

What does it cost?

₹55,000–₹95,000 per flat including lock, hub, sensors, AC control and commissioning. The model above shows the payback on your rates, not ours.

What about parties?

A decibel sensor can warn you when a flat is loud at 2 a.m., and who is inside can be inferred from the door and presence events. It is evidence for a conversation, not a surveillance system.
